Case Study · adidas

Order history

Clientadidas
RoleProduct designer

Order History was just a list, but users could see their orders, but couldn't do anything from there. I redesigned it into a clear, self-service entry point, driving self-service returns up 22% and helping cut CS calls about existing orders by 8–10%.

Situation & Problem

7 million visits. 64% still called support.

Order History was one of adidas's highest-traffic post-sales entry points, yet most users couldn't resolve their needs independently, and the most critical information was buried.

[IMAGEN — captura del navegador con Order History]

Fragmented across platforms

Each platform evolved independently, creating inconsistent patterns, information hierarchy and available actions.

Task

Transform Order History into the foundation of adidas's entire post-sales self service.

Redesign Order History into a self-service entry point that lets users take action directly, reducing the need to contact CS.

  • 01
    Track an existing orderDelivery date and status, front and center.
  • 02
    Modify or cancel an orderSelf-service before it ships.
  • 03
    Find information about previous ordersStatus, details, refund, return, without contact.
  • 04
    Initiate a return or exchangeHighest-friction action, most common future need.

Two constraints from day one

01 Phased delivery

Engineering couldn't build everything at once → Led to a modular, state-driven component model.

02 No behavioral data

Without real data on user behavior, every design decision would be self-key → Led to research-first, principle-led decision making.

Actions

A modular card. Three zones. One system.

I introduced a modular card structure (When / What / Action) with state-driven CTAs and a color-coded urgency system, mapping every design decision to a specific research finding.

[IMAGEN — online card drop, 3 zonas]

Online card drop

Results & Outcome

8–10% Reduction in post-sales customer contacts (CTO)

Customers could finally take action on their own instead of calling for help. Making the return option easier to find drove self-service returns up 22%, so people who could see the action they needed right away completed it themselves.

The same design shipped across web, mobile web, and app, so every customer got the same experience regardless of platform. One solution, used in three places, helped more customers solve their own problems.

+22%

Returns redirected to self-service by surfacing the action at the right level.
